UNDERSTANDING THE RED SHADED AREA

The red zone marks where cumulative operator-hours removed by cuts exceeds operator-hours freed by AI. Past that boundary, the practice can't deliver the call volume with remaining staff.

For each person cut, in order: cumulative hours removed += person's operator hours/year
The threshold: red shading begins where cumulative hours removed > freed hours

Example at current settings: AI frees 10,388 hours/yr. Each path crosses into the red zone at a different point because HVAs and onsite PSAs contribute different operator-hour amounts when cut. Two paths are shown so you can see how the order of cuts affects when you hit the wall.

How this model works

  • Whole-person savings. Each cut saves the departing person's full annual wages (total hours × hourly rate × 52). Their full role goes away, not just the operator portion.
  • Cut order. Highest operator-allocation employees first — the people whose work AI most directly replaces. See Section 06 for the per-person breakdown that drives the cut order.
  • Stranded capacity. When AI frees more hours than your cuts remove, the unused freed time becomes idle slack with no savings credit. This is honest accounting — you only save what you actually stop spending.
  • Non-operator work. Letting go of someone with 50% operator allocation also eliminates their 50% non-operator work (front desk, supplies, etc.). The model assumes this gets absorbed by remaining team — review feasibility per location.
04 / The Revenue Upside

Missed calls are the silent revenue leak.

At 3–5% missed-call rate during office hours, the practice loses ~760–950 inbound calls per month that EliseAI could potentially capture. Most are existing patients trying to book appointments. Off-hours calls are excluded from this analysis to keep the math conservative.

Conservative assumptions explained

  • Office-hours missed (3–5%). Your stated current performance. Industry average for medical practices is 20–38%, so the team is already strong here — meaning office-hours recovery upside is smaller than typical case studies suggest.
  • Off-hours excluded. Calls coming in after office hours are not modeled. EliseAI's 24/7 availability could theoretically capture those too, but without phone log data on after-hours volume and intent, including them would inflate the projection. This is an upside not counted here.
  • Conversion rate (10% default). Of calls captured by AI that would otherwise be missed, what % result in an actual booked-and-attended visit. The default is intentionally conservative: most missed calls already get returned by the team (callback workflow), so the truly "lost" calls are smaller than the raw missed rate suggests. Of those, some won't convert at all, and of those that book, ~10–20% no-show. Slide to 0% to model the case where AI captures no incremental revenue (callback team is fully effective).
  • Revenue per visit ($120). Your stated average. Note this is per-visit, not lifetime — so the model is conservative versus practices that measure patient lifetime value.
  • Why this number is fuzzy. Without data on new-patient call %, lifetime value, or conversion rates, the revenue figure has wider error bars than the labor numbers. Treat as an upper-bound indicator, not a forecast.

Caveats before signing anything

  • Revenue upside is theoretical. Recovered revenue assumes AI actually captures the missed calls AND those calls convert at the assumed rate. Validate with a 90-day pilot before counting on it.
  • Labor savings require staff action. If you don't actually let go of the people the slider says, savings = $0 and AI cost is purely additive. Pilot before promising headcount reductions.
  • Implementation cost not modeled. Typical AI-receptionist integrations require 4–8 weeks of setup, parallel-run with humans, and ongoing tuning. Budget $20–40K Year 1 for implementation, training, and oversight.
  • EliseAI vendor risk. Pricing is per-resolved-call which aligns incentives but exposes the practice to vendor price increases. Negotiate price-lock or cap clauses.
  • Per-call economics. At ~11 calls/hour, a human operator costs $2.19/call (onsite) or $0.87/call (HVA). EliseAI at $1.15/call is cheaper than onsite, more expensive than HVA — which is why the staff mix decision drives the outcome.
