SPO still generates positive ROI — but investigate before deciding
SPO Booking is not losing money at $90 net ROI per patient. However, it delivers 35% lower ROI than standard Marketplace at 48% higher cost, with lower visit rates and higher no-show rates. Before pausing, three questions should be answered: (1) Does SPO generate meaningful incremental volume that wouldn't otherwise come through Marketplace? (2) Are there specific providers or locations where SPO performs better? (3) Is the lower quality driven by a specific insurance or patient mix? The data available does not isolate these factors — they warrant investigation before a final call.

⚠ Self Pay — Interpret with Caution
Many patients classified as "Self Pay" in ZocDoc are not uninsured — they simply did not provide insurance information at the time of booking. The metrics for this group likely reflect a mix of true self-pay and patients whose insurance was captured later in the EHR. Cross-reference with ModMed collections data before drawing conclusions about this segment's true value.
Government Patients — The Conversion Problem
38.2% of Medicaid/Medicare patients never complete a visit — the highest zero-activity rate of any group. But once they attend, they visit frequently (2.16 visits in 3M) and their LTV/paying patient is $282 — nearly identical to Commercial. The problem is pre-visit friction, not patient value. Better reminder flows and eligibility verification for this segment would directly improve channel ROI.

The Planning Multiplier
Use 3-month cohort for all current operational decisions — it's valid for all 3,240 patients across all of 2025.

Apply a 1.57× multiplier for annual planning: a 3M LTV of $200 implies a true 12M LTV of approximately $314. Use this when modeling Branded Directory ROI and long-term channel value.

Do not compare Q2–Q4 12-month figures — those windows are incomplete. The declining uplift pattern visible in the Power BI 12M report (Q1 +57%, Q4 +13%) is entirely a timing artifact, not a real trend.
Final Chapter

What to do next.

Six actions, in priority order.

1
High priority
Conduct Branded Directory trial study and make decision
The trial ends May 2026. Collect April and May booking data and run the channel-separated break-even (Marketplace vs BD independently) before committing to $67,500/year. The exit risk — losing ~283 previously-free bookings/month permanently — likely exceeds the flat fee, but two months of data are needed to confirm volume is structural rather than seasonal. Also evaluate whether alternative online booking platforms such as Klara, NexHealth, or Podium could serve a similar function at a different cost structure.
2
High priority
Establish a sustainable periodic data pipeline
The current analysis relied on manual data exports from ZocDoc and ModMed. A repeatable monthly process — scheduled exports, consistent file formats, and a defined update workflow into Power BI — is needed to maintain this analysis over time without re-building it from scratch each cycle. Define the cadence, ownership, and format before the next data refresh.
3
High priority
Implement ZocDoc Tier 2/3 link optimization sitewide
Free channel patients convert at 75.9% vs 63.8% for Marketplace. Website-referred patients (GuestCheckout) converted at 63% in the BD dashboard — 4.4× the overall average. Location-specific and provider-specific ZocDoc links directly amplify the highest-quality patient source. All URLs are documented in ClickUp.
4
High priority
Investigate SPO Booking before pausing
SPO still generates positive ROI ($90/patient). Before reducing spend, determine: Does SPO bring incremental volume that wouldn't come through standard Marketplace? Are there specific providers or locations where SPO performs better? Is the lower visit rate driven by a specific patient mix?
5
Medium priority
Reduce Government patient pre-visit friction
38.2% of Medicaid/Medicare patients never complete a visit. Once they attend, their LTV/paying is nearly identical to Commercial ($282 vs $291). Targeted confirmation reminders and eligibility verification for this segment could materially lift conversion without changing acquisition cost.
6
Medium priority
Segment high-value patients by provider and location
288 patients generated 40% of revenue at an avg of 4.7 visits in 3 months. Fort Lee (32% of BD bookings) and Ra La Huh / Sophy Yu (highest confirmation rates) are natural starting points. Understanding what drives these patients is the highest-value remaining analysis.
