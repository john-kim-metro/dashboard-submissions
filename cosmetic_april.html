Office of the CIO/CSO — April 2026 Cosmetic Review

Author: John Kim · Reporting period: April 2026 · Prepared: May 11, 2026 · Status: Confidential
Companion analysis to the Cosmetic Volume & No-Show Dashboard
April broke the cosmetic slump. After 15 consecutive months of running below the prior year, April 2026 delivered 1,227 completed visits — the highest single month in the available 28-month dataset and the first month with positive year-over-year growth (+16.4% vs April 2025). The recovery is driven primarily by an injectables surge and an all-time-high new patient count of 128, both traceable to a Saturday Jeuveau / Letybo event held on April 18 at Fort Lee. Bronx Concourse, by contrast, continues its structural decline.

1Injectables — specifically Cosmetic Botox — drove the rebound

Of the seven cosmetic service categories, Injectables had been the worst-performing throughout 2025 — down 20% year-over-year in March. In April, it became the largest contributor to growth. Cosmetic Botox alone went from 118 visits in March to 203 in April, a 72% month-over-month jump and 57% above April 2025.

The surge is concentrated in Botox, but every injectables sub-type grew in both directions — there is no offsetting decline elsewhere in the category. The driver is identifiable: a Saturday afternoon Jeuveau and Letybo event held on April 18th. This single event is the most likely explanation for the +85-visit MoM jump in Cosmetic Botox, with smaller spillover into Filler and PRP as patients combined services during the visit.

What this tells us: A single Saturday event meaningfully moved the category — Cosmetic Botox alone added ~85 visits versus March, on a base of 118. If similar events were held quarterly, even with diminishing per-event returns, the annualized lift could substantially exceed April's one-month bump. The cost of the event versus the marginal revenue from added Botox, Filler, and PRP visits should be characterized so future scheduling can be optimized.

2New cosmetic patients hit an all-time high — and it traces back to the same event

April delivered 128 new cosmetic patients, up from 81 in March (+58% MoM) and 96 a year ago (+33% YoY). This is the highest single month for new cosmetic patient acquisition in the 28-month dataset — the previous peak was 99 in March 2025.

The location pattern matches the Botox finding closely. Fort Lee, where the Jeuveau / Letybo event was held, more than doubled its 2024 baseline — 47 new patients in April versus 20 in April 2024 (+135% over two years). Flushing Northern also nearly doubled month-over-month, suggesting either spillover bookings or coordinated promotion across the two top-volume sites.

This is the more durable story. Botox volume reflects the immediate transaction lift; new patient acquisition is what determines whether the lift compounds. A new cosmetic patient typically generates 3–6 visits in the first year across consults, follow-ups, and additional services. The 47 net incremental new patients added in April (versus the 28-month average of 77) represent a forward revenue pipeline materially larger than the single-month injectables transaction value. The next two months will reveal what share of these new patients return for second visits — that retention rate determines the true ROI of the event.

3Fort Lee is now a category of its own

Fort Lee completed 412 cosmetic visits in April — a record for any cosmetic location in the dataset. It now accounts for 34% of total cosmetic volume, up from 28% a year ago. The growth is not a one-month event: Fort Lee has been on a near-continuous upward trajectory for 18 months.

Fort Lee also maintains the lowest no-show rate in the cosmetic network at 3.7% — meaning the volume is converting cleanly to revenue, not being padded by inflated booking. The location is operating efficiently at its current size, but the question of capacity ceiling becomes relevant: if growth continues at +44% YoY, Fort Lee will need either scheduling expansion or panel transfer to neighboring sites.

What this means: Fort Lee is the cosmetic flagship and should be studied as a model — what scheduling templates, provider mix, and service mix are driving the result? Glen Oaks (+42% YoY in only its second year) is showing the same shape on a smaller base, suggesting the pattern may be replicable rather than location-specific.

4Bronx Concourse cosmetic is in structural decline

While the rest of the cosmetic network has recovered, Bronx Concourse continues to fall. April delivered 139 visits — down 24.9% year-over-year and 37.7% over two years. This is the same pattern observed on the medical side at the same location.

Bronx Concourse cosmetic — monthly volume (Jan 2024 – Apr 2026)
A consistent downward slope. No seasonal cycle is offsetting the trend.

The no-show rate also remains elevated at 10.3% — three times the rate at the other locations. Bronx Concourse is the only cosmetic site with a no-show rate above 7%, and it has held in the 9–17% range for the entire 28-month window.

This is consistent with the medical-side finding. Both the medical and cosmetic patient panels at Bronx Concourse are eroding, while Bronx Fordham (medical) and the other cosmetic locations are gaining. A patient-level analysis should confirm whether Bronx Concourse patients are: (1) leaving the system entirely, (2) being rerouted to Fordham for medical and to Fort Lee or Glen Oaks for cosmetic, or (3) not being booked at all due to a scheduling or staffing constraint. The diagnosis matters because the remedy is different for each.

5Cancellation rate dropped to the lowest level on record

April's cancellation rate was 22.5% — down 2.5 percentage points from March and the lowest figure in the entire 28-month dataset. The previous best was 22.5% in May 2025. For 27 of 28 months prior, cancellations have held in the 22.6–28.0% band.

Cosmetic cancellation rate — monthly trend (Jan 2024 – Apr 2026)
28 months in a tight 22.5–28% band. April 2026 broke through the lower bound.

Cancellation has been the cosmetic practice's largest demand-leakage source — at 25% baseline, roughly one in four booked appointments never converts. A 2.5pp improvement at the system level represents about 35 additional preserved bookings per month, or ~420 per year.

Whether this is signal or noise is unclear from one month. The April number could reflect a real change in operational discipline (deposit policy, reminder cadence, lead-time management) or a temporary effect of the unusually strong April booking pull. May's reading will indicate which. If cancellations stay below 24% for two consecutive months, the improvement should be characterized and the contributing change identified.

Areas to inspect

  1. Characterize the April 18 Jeuveau / Letybo event ROI. Quantify the marginal revenue lift from the event (Cosmetic Botox +85 visits MoM, plus Filler and PRP spillover) against the event cost. Establish whether a quarterly or bi-monthly cadence of similar events would deliver predictable category growth, and identify which provider mix and time slot performed best.
  2. Track retention of the April new patient cohort. 128 new cosmetic patients in April is the all-time monthly high — but the long-term value depends on what share return for second and third visits. Set up a 60- and 90-day follow-up cohort analysis to measure return rate, average services per returning patient, and revenue per acquired patient. This is the data needed to underwrite future event spend.
  3. Study Fort Lee as a model. Document the scheduling templates, provider mix, service mix, and operational practices driving the +44% YoY growth. Apply the relevant elements to Flushing Northern and Glen Oaks where the pattern is starting to emerge.
  4. Plan for Fort Lee capacity. At 412 visits/month and growing, Fort Lee is approaching the upper bound of what the current footprint can support. Determine whether the next 12 months require scheduling expansion, additional providers, or panel transfer to relieve pressure.
  5. Investigate Bronx Concourse decline causes. A 37.7% two-year volume drop with consistent 10%+ no-show rate suggests either panel attrition, provider departures, or geographic shift to neighboring sites. The same investigation should be coordinated with the medical-side Concourse review — both panels are showing the same pattern at the same location.
  6. Watch the cancellation rate in May. If May 2026 cancellation rate comes in below 24%, the April improvement is structural and the contributing operational change should be documented. If it returns to 25%+, April was noise.
