Office of the CIO/CSO — June 2026 Cosmetic Review

Author: John Kim · Reporting period: June 2026 · Prepared: July 13, 2026 · Status: Confidential
Companion analysis to the Cosmetic Volume & No-Show Dashboard

1Cosmetic volume recovered to above its normal June band

Cosmetic completed visits rose to 1,065 in June, up 7.7% from May and 8.0% above June 2025. That places June above the normal 2024/2025 June range (917 and 986 respectively) and continues the healthy 2026 run. After May's post-event normalization, June shows the underlying demand is not just stable but growing year-over-year.

The operating-day calendar slightly flatters the raw MoM figure: June had 24.0 operating-day-equivalents versus May's 22.5 (Memorial Day). On a visits-per-operating-day basis (VPD, shown on the dashboard), the MoM gain narrows from +7.7% to about +1.0% (44.0 to 44.4 VPD), while the YoY gain is +3.5%. So the month-over-month jump is mostly calendar, but the year-over-year gain is real underlying growth — the cleaner signal here.

Through six months, 2026 cosmetic volume is running ahead of 2025. The April event spike, May's giveback, and June's above-trend recovery net out to a first half that is clearly stronger year-over-year — the growth is broad, not concentrated in the one event month.

The recovery confirms durable growth. A June that comes in above both prior Junes — on a per-operating-day basis as well as raw — shows the cosmetic line is on a genuine upward trajectory, not riding a single event. Cancellation rate also fell to a series-low 22.0%, reinforcing that the demand is high-quality.

2Injectables led the recovery, up 29% year-over-year

Injectables reached 173 completed visits in June, up 8.1% MoM and 29.1% YoY — the strongest year-over-year gain of any cosmetic category. Cosmetic Botox specifically came in at 135, above the year-ago 112 and comfortably in the upper half of its historical range. The category has clearly stepped up from its 2024–2025 baseline.

The +29% YoY injectables gain is the clearest sign that the April Jeuveau/Letybo event left a durable footprint. Rather than a spike-and-collapse, injectables have settled at a structurally higher level two months later — consistent with the event having converted genuinely new patients who are now returning for maintenance treatments.

The event's durable payoff is now visible. Two months after the April event, injectables are running nearly a third above last year. That is exactly the pattern that justifies a repeat: not the one-month spike, but the sustained higher baseline afterward. If injectables hold near this level through Q3, the case for a quarterly event cadence is well supported.

3New patient acquisition reached a strong year-over-year gain

New cosmetic patients came in at 109 in June, up 12.4% MoM and 18.5% YoY. That is well above the year-ago 92 and near the upper end of the historical range, second only to April's event-driven record of 128. The acquisition engine did not exhaust itself after the April event — it has settled at a durably higher level.

New cosmetic patients are the most durable growth signal, because each typically generates several visits over the following year. A June that runs 18.5% above last year — sustained two months past the event — suggests the higher acquisition rate is becoming the new normal rather than an event artifact. The compounding effect of these patients returning for maintenance is what is now showing up in the injectables line above.

Acquisition has stepped up structurally. The April cohort has now had time to begin returning, and the elevated new-patient and injectables numbers together suggest the event genuinely expanded the patient base rather than pulling demand forward. Continuing to track the April/May cohorts' second- and third-visit rates remains the key ROI measurement, but the top-line trend is clearly positive.

4The location split persists: Fort Lee at a new high, Bronx Concourse weak

The two structural location stories continued and sharpened. Fort Lee rose to 357 visits, a new high — up 8.5% MoM and 41.7% YoY, now clearly the network's cosmetic growth engine. Bronx Concourse recovered modestly to 139 (from May's 122) but remains far below its year-ago level, continuing the multi-year decline that mirrors the medical-side pattern at the same location.

Fort Lee's 41.7% YoY gain is now the dominant cosmetic story — the site has grown from 252 to 357 in a year with a near-zero no-show rate (1.7%), a model of high-quality demand. At the other end, Bronx Concourse remains down 21% YoY with a 16.3% no-show rate, by far the worst in the cosmetic network. Concourse is the one location where the decline is clearly structural.

Bronx Concourse remains the cross-service concern. Both the cosmetic and medical panels at this location are eroding simultaneously — cosmetic down 21% YoY, medical no-show pinned at 28.5%. This is a site-level problem, not a service-line problem. A single coordinated review of Concourse (staffing, provider continuity, local competition, patient migration) would serve both service lines better than two separate investigations. This is the same recommendation as last month, now more urgent given Fort Lee shows what a healthy site looks like.
