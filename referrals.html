Referral Trend Analysis  ·  Baseline for Q3 Strategy

Are our top referrers really declining?

With cosmetic locations removed and overall visit volume as the control, the picture is unambiguous: the practice is busier than ever, yet our top referrers are sending fewer patients. The two move in opposite directions.

Scope: Medical (MED) locations only Metric: Unique Patients Referred Window: Jan–Apr, 2024 / 2025 / 2026 Control: Completed Visits (ModMed)
The Verdict

Top-referrer decline is independent of — and opposite to — general visit volume.

Completed medical visits are up 8.4% since 2024 (Jan–Apr). Over the same window, our top 20 referrers fell 19.6% and the top 100 fell 11%. If the drop were just a quieter practice, referrals and visits would fall together. Instead the practice grew while anchor referrals shrank — so this is relationship-specific erosion, exactly what a dedicated referral effort is built to reverse. It is not explained by patient demand.

Referral intensity is falling

Fewer anchor referrals per patient seen

Normalizing referrals against visit volume removes any doubt. For every 1,000 completed visits, our top 20 referrers accounted for 59 referred patients in 2024 — but only 44 in 2026, a 26% drop in referral intensity. Even adjusted for a busier practice, the anchors are contributing proportionally less.

03

A shrinking share of the whole

How much the top 20 still carry

In 2024 the top 20 providers supplied 27.5% of all medical referrals — more than one in four. By 2026 that fell to 21.3%. The practice is steadily de-concentrating away from its anchor relationships: a more diversified base in one sense, but also a loss of the high-trust, high-volume partnerships that are hardest to replace and most worth defending.

Where the volume went

From anchors to the long tail

Within referrals, volume redistributed. The top 20 fell and ranks 21–100 fell, while the long tail of smaller referrers grew and absorbed the difference. More offices refer occasionally; our cultivated relationships refer less.

07

What this means for Q3

From diagnosis to action

1 · Win-back is the right theme

The decline is relationship-specific and reversible — not a demand problem. Re-engaging lapsed anchors is the highest-leverage Q3 focus.

2 · Geography sets the route

Concentrate early visits around Flushing Northern and Fort Lee referrers, where the loss is steepest and the volume largest.

3 · Study the growers

A handful of top providers grew despite the trend. Learning why is as valuable as diagnosing the decline.

4 · Intensity is the metric to watch

Referrals per 1,000 visits cuts through practice growth. Track it monthly — it is the cleanest read on whether outreach is working.

Measurement caveatsReferral figures cover only appointments with a referring provider recorded (~19% of all). Completed-visit totals are medical-only, matching the referral scope exactly. Improving front-desk capture of the referring-provider field remains essential.
