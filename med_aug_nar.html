Metro Dermatology — August 2026 Medical Review

Author: Darell Dado· Reporting period: August 2026· Prepared: September 11, 2026· Status: Confidential
Companion analysis to the Medical Volume & No-Show dashboard and the Medical Productivity dashboard — see section P for the corrected hours basis.
July's conversion problem partly corrected itself, and this month there is no calendar effect hiding anything. August and July both carried 23.5 operating-day-equivalents, so every move below is real rather than a by-product of days open. Completed medical visits were 12,998, effectively flat on July (−0.3%) and −0.9% against August 2025. Underneath that flat line, demand fell −3.4% while both failure rates improved — no-show 20.0% → 19.2% and cancellation 26.8% → 25.2%. The practice converted a better share of a smaller book. The year-on-year picture is less comfortable and is the one worth taking to the meeting.

1Fewer bookings, better conversion — the exact inverse of July

July read as a demand story meeting a failing funnel: bookings up, conversion down, cancellations at a 2026 high. August is the mirror image. Total appointment demand fell to 21,521 from 22,286, a decline of −3.4%. Because operating-days are unchanged, demand per operating-day fell by exactly the same −3.4%, from 948.3 to 915.8.

Completed visits barely moved — 12,998 against 13,042. That is only possible because conversion improved. -546 cancellations and -178 no-shows against July means roughly 724 appointments that would have fallen through in July did not fall through in August. That almost exactly offsets the drop in bookings.

The July cancellation spike was a one-month event, not the start of a trend. Cancellation rate peaked at 26.8% in July — the highest month of 2026 — and fell back 1.6 points in August. On the evidence of two months, July looks like an outlier rather than a step change. One more month at or below 25.2% would settle it.

2The year-on-year conversion decline is the durable finding

Month-to-month, August looks like a recovery. Against last August it does not. Demand is +3.9% year-on-year while completed visits are −0.9%. More people are asking for appointments and fewer of them are being seen.

Conversion is the cleanest way to state it: 63.3% in August 2025, 60.4% in August 2026 — a loss of 2.9 points. Applied to August's demand of 21,521, closing that gap back to last year's rate would have produced roughly 627 additional completed visits in the month, without a single extra booking. The 2026 year-to-date average sits at 59.3% against 61.5% for 2025.

3A flat system total hides a 539-visit swing — but two sites need a calendar correction first

Two single-site closures distort the location comparison, and they land on the two biggest movers. The system was open every scheduled day in August, so the headline figures above are unaffected. But Fort Lee was closed on Friday 24 July and Flushing Northern had no provider on Saturday 29 August. Each site therefore needs its own operating-day count, not the system's 23.5:
  • Fort Lee — July ODE 22.5, August 23.5. Raw +13.6% becomes +8.7% per operating-day.
  • Flushing Northern — July ODE 23.5, August 23.0. Raw −7.5% becomes −5.5% per operating-day.
The Flushing closure is recorded in the holiday and closure calendar ("No Provider", Flushing). The Fort Lee closure is not on that calendar — it was found in the daily data (0 completed visits against 7 booked, versus 119–154 on every other July Friday) and should be added.

The system moved -44 visits. That number is the net of two sites gaining +496 and four sites losing -540 — a gross swing of 1,036 visits that is entirely invisible at system level. On a like-for-like per-operating-day basis the spread runs from +9.7% to -6.9%.

The correction changes the ranking. Flushing Northern looked like the month's worst site on raw numbers; adjusted, Bronx Concourse is the worst decliner at −6.9%, with Flushing second at −5.5%. Fort Lee remains the strongest site, but roughly a third of its apparent gain was the July closure rather than August growth.

Two independent books falling together at one location is a site-level signal, not a service-line one. This has now been flagged for three consecutive cycles as an observation; it should be escalated to a scheduled site review.

4Procedures remain 17% below last August — still unexplained

Procedures recovered +2.2% on the month to 2,073 visits, but sit −16.7% against August 2025. This is the same gap flagged in the June and July cycles and it has not closed. The category is dominated by AS SAAS, Excimer Laser and phototherapy — treatment types whose volume is driven by ordering patterns and payer authorisation rules rather than by patient demand.

The open question from previous cycles is unchanged: is this a permanent payer-rule change, in which case the current level is the new baseline and capacity planning should be reset to it, or a change in SAAS ordering behaviour, which is clinically addressable? The Fordham and Flushing Northern drill-down proposed several cycles ago remains the fastest way to separate the two.

5ZocDoc: fourth month with Sponsored Results at zero — the ad cut is settled

August produced 874 total ZocDoc bookings and 523 new patients, with Sponsored Results at zero for the fourth consecutive month. Total spend was $6,380, all of it marketplace booking fees and none of it advertising.

The honest read is that volume has plateaued rather than continued recovering. Bookings have sat in a narrow 872–900 band for four months and new patients in a 505–532 band. August is marginally below July on both. Against the pre-cut April level (985 total, 696 new) the practice is running about 11% lower on bookings and 25% lower on new patients — but at zero advertising cost. Four months of stable volume is enough to call this settled: the ads were buying incremental new patients at a price the practice has decided not to pay, and the underlying marketplace volume is holding without them.

PProductivity is included this cycle

The blocker cleared on 11 September; the productivity dashboard ships with this cycle. Templated hours had been frozen at a ~1 June 2026 snapshot for three cycles. They now come from the live daily schedule, and the reporting model has been rebuilt and verified against it. The staleness symptoms are gone: August reports 2,013 templated hours and real no-show counts, not zero.

Two things changed at once, and both matter for reading August against July:

  1. The VPH visit count moved to the appointment record. Provider visit counts now come from completed appointments, attributed to the provider who saw the patient, rather than from booked-appointment counts carried on the scheduling extract, with the seven treatment-only appointment types excluded. The two sources agree on visit counts to 0.01% across all 24 months.
  2. The hours denominator is live. The frozen template’s forward months subtracted no leave, holidays or schedule changes — running roughly 14% high.
July’s published VPH was understated. On the corrected hours July 2026 is 5.50, not the 5.23 published last cycle, and the June → July move was −4.3%, not the −9.9% reported. The 24-month production average moves from 5.07 to 5.12. Every month in the productivity series therefore differs from the July file. Volume, no-show and cancellation figures in this report are unaffected — they have always come live from the appointment record. August system VPH is 5.37, −2.4% on July and +1.1% year-on-year, on 2,013 templated hours across 18 active providers.

What to do with this

  1. Ask Bronx Fordham what changed — it is the cleanest result of the month. +9.7% per operating-day with the no-show rate down 2.9 points from the worst base in the system, with no calendar effect and no reported driver. Ask Fort Lee too (+8.7% per operating-day).
  2. Add the Fort Lee closure of Friday 24 July to the holiday and closure calendar. It is absent from that calendar, which is what the operating-day count is built from. Any month with an unrecorded single-site closure produces a wrong per-site comparison in both directions — this one moved Fort Lee by 4.8 points and changed which site ranked worst.
  3. Escalate Bronx Concourse from observation to site review. Both service lines are falling together at one site across three cycles. Medical is −10.5% YoY and cosmetic −34.5% YoY.
  4. Treat conversion, not volume, as the headline metric. Completed visits have been flat for three months and say little. Conversion fell from 63.3% to 60.4% year-on-year, worth roughly 627 visits a month at current demand.
  5. Close the Procedures question. −16.7% YoY has persisted for three cycles without a decision on whether it is a permanent payer-rule change or an ordering-behaviour change. The Fordham / Flushing Northern drill-down answers it.
  6. Review Sit’s schedule before the September cycle. Three consecutive monthly VPH falls, 4.80 to 4.15, back under the 4.5 Month-12 milestone cleared in April. August is not a denominator effect — system templated hours rose 1.4% while her VPH fell 3.0%. Garcia stays on the intervention flag at 3.34, still under the 3.5 Month-6 target.
