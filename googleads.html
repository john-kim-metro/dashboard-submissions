§ 04 — Three reasons the relationship should end

Not one reason — three, each sufficient on its own.

i.

The unit economics don't work.

At $312 per visited patient vs. $51 at Concourse, the vendor costs 6× more per acquisition. Even our worst in-house campaign (Telehealth, $91) is 3× cheaper than the vendor.

Vendor blended: $311.63 / NP visited
In-house blended: ~$73 / NP visited
ii.

The trend is deteriorating.

Performance peaked in July 2025 and has steadily declined. The last two months (Mar–Apr 2026) show cost per booking back above $500 — among the worst on record. A 21-month campaign should be at its best, not its worst.

Jul '25 cost/booking: $50
Apr '26 cost/booking: $554
iii.

We've proven we can fix campaigns ourselves.

Glen Oaks was misconfigured for click maximization through March. We pivoted to call optimization in April. In a single month, cost per visited new patient dropped from $365 to $73 — an 80% improvement.

Glen Oaks Mar '26: $365 / NP visited
Glen Oaks Apr '26: $73 / NP visited

§ 06 — Before terminating

Two questions worth asking first.

The case is strong, but document it cleanly. Two short conversations will protect us when the vendor pushes back.

i.

Ask the vendor what objective their campaign is optimized for.

If they answer "clicks" or "awareness," the conversation is short — we're paying acquisition prices for traffic. If they describe a strategic role (brand defense, competitor conquesting, top-of-funnel awareness), that may justify the metric gap — but should be evaluated separately.

ii.

Confirm the comparison is apples-to-apples.

The vendor may be targeting different geographies, services, or audiences than our location campaigns. Validate that they're competing for the same patient before terminating — but the C2C gap (3.08% vs. 45.93%) is too large to be explained by targeting alone.

iii.

Document this analysis and share before the cancellation call.

The vendor will push back. The side-by-side numbers make the conversation short and the decision defensible. Save this report — and the underlying PowerBI data — in the Campaign Diary.
