Top 30 Diagnoses by Frequency

Prepared for: CEO · Scope: Medical dermatology, evaluation visits · Period: Jan 2024 – May 2026
Measured in diagnosis count (Dx count) — how often each condition is coded.
This report ranks the conditions the practice diagnoses most often. The book is highly concentrated: the top 30 diagnoses account for 63% of all diagnosis activity, and Acne Vulgaris alone is roughly 1 in 7. The mix is growing on the right things — dermatitis, skin-cancer screening, and hair-loss diagnoses are rising fastest.

Methodology — why these numbers, and what's excluded

We count diagnoses, not visits. A single patient visit can carry more than one diagnosis (for example, acne and seborrheic dermatitis assessed in the same appointment). This report therefore measures diagnosis count (Dx count) — the number of times each condition was coded — which is the correct unit for ranking what the practice diagnoses. It is not a count of unique visits or unique patients, and Dx counts should not be summed across different diagnoses to infer visit volume.

We filtered to evaluation (E&M) visits only, excluding treatment encounters. Treatment visits — phototherapy, excimer laser, and similar recurring procedures — are coded against a diagnosis but are not new clinical assessments. A patient receiving phototherapy several times a week generates many treatment records for the same underlying condition, which would inflate that condition's apparent frequency. By keeping only E&M (evaluation and decision-making) encounters, the ranking reflects genuine diagnostic activity rather than the cadence of a treatment program. The clearest example: under an all-encounters view, vitiligo appeared to be a top-5 condition, but roughly 5 of every 6 of those records were phototherapy treatments — on an evaluation basis it ranks far lower.
