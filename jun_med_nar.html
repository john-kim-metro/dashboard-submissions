Office of the CIO/CSO — June 2026 Medical & Productivity Review

Author: John Kim · Reporting period: June 2026 · Prepared: July 13, 2026 · Status: Confidential
Companion analysis to the Medical Volume & No-Show and Medical Productivity dashboards

1June delivered the rebound May's per-day rate predicted — but the calendar did much of the work

Completed visits rose to 13,353 in June, up 8.9% from May and 3.0% above June 2025. That headline jump overstates the underlying gain, though, because June had more operating days than May: no closures this month, against May's Memorial Day. Counting weekdays as 1.0 and Saturdays as 0.5, June 2026 had 24.0 operating-day-equivalents versus 22.5 in May and 23.0 in June 2025.

On that like-for-like basis, June ran 556 visits per operating-day (VPD) — up a more measured 2.0% from May's 545, and 1.3% below June 2025's 564. So the demand recovery is real but modest: most of the 8.9% raw jump is the extra working days, not a surge in patients. Last month's report predicted this rebound — May's strong per-day rate implied the raw total would mechanically recover toward ~13,000 once the calendar normalized, and June landed slightly above that.

Because June carried a fuller calendar than May, the operating-day drag reverses this month: where a category or site below shows a strong month-over-month gain, roughly 6 points of it is simply the added capacity. The residual after that adjustment is the real movement. Total appointment demand (completed + no-show + cancelled) rose 6.9% MoM and 5.3% YoY to 22,159 — a genuine YoY demand gain that the per-day view confirms.

What this means: June is a healthy month, but read it on a per-day basis. Underlying demand grew about 2% over May and is running close to last year on capacity-adjusted throughput. The practice is stable and near its recent ceiling; the raw 13,353 is a calendar-flattered version of a solid-but-modest 2% per-day gain.

2The no-show rate held near its high — and Bronx Concourse rebounded without fixing it

June's system no-show rate was 20.1%, essentially flat from May's 20.2% and still the second-highest reading in the dataset back to January 2023. Because this is a ratio, the calendar has no effect on it — the near-record level is a clean, real signal. June is now the ninth consecutive month above 18%. Whatever is driving the structural drift did not ease this month even as volume recovered.

Bronx Concourse rebounded sharply on volume — up 16.8% MoM to 2,714 completed — yet its no-show rate stayed pinned at 28.5%, the second-worst in the network behind Fordham. That combination is the concern: the site recovered its booking volume, but the recovered bookings are just as unreliable as before. Both Bronx sites remain above 28% while every other location sits below 20%. The two Bronx locations continue to hold essentially the entire network no-show problem.

Volume recovery is masking an unfixed no-show problem at Concourse. The site looks healthier on the volume dashboard this month, but nearly 3 in 10 of its booked visits still go unattended. A volume rebound with an unchanged 28.5% no-show rate means the underlying panel-reliability issue is untouched — the extra bookings simply carry the same failure rate. This deserves a targeted reminder/confirmation intervention at Concourse specifically, separate from Fordham's overbooking-style pattern.

3Procedures ticked up month-over-month, but the year-over-year decline is now nine months deep

Procedures reached 2,028 completed visits in June, up 3.9% from May — but that MoM gain is almost entirely the extra operating days, and against June 2025 the category is still down 21.1% year-over-year. This extends the structural decline documented since late 2025 into its ninth month. The per-day view removes the small MoM bump and leaves the YoY erosion clearly intact.

SAAS remains the largest drag at −25.9% YoY, roughly 1,021 this June against 1,378 a year ago. As established in prior months, SAAS is recorded under "Non-Provider, Treatment" so the ordering provider is not visible in the appointment data, and the decline concentrates at Bronx Fordham and Flushing Northern. The recommended EMA ordering-provider drill-down at those two sites remains the open action item carried since April; nine months of sustained decline makes it the most overdue item in this report.

This is now a sustained, material revenue line. SAAS has fallen from roughly 1,380/month a year ago to about 1,020 today, and the laser sub-types are all down double digits YoY. Combined, procedures run about 540 visits/month below their year-ago level. The SAAS portion is not explained by payer rules and remains the most actionable piece — if the ordering-pattern shift can be reversed, it is recoverable volume. The drill-down should not slip another month.

4ZocDoc new patients recovered with ads still off — weakening the ad-cut worry

Last month flagged a concern: ZocDoc new-patient bookings had dropped 27% in May (696 → 505), the same month Sponsored Ads were switched off, and the leading hypothesis was that cutting the ads caused the decline. June resolves much of that worry. With Sponsored Ads still at zero, new-patient bookings recovered to 525 (up from 505) and total bookings rose to 889 (from 872). The feared continued slide did not happen.

That recovery is the decisive data point. If cutting the ads had genuinely removed ~80–90 incremental new patients a month, June — a second full month with no ad spend — should have stayed depressed or fallen further. Instead the channel ticked back up on its own. The most likely reading now is that May's dip was largely noise, not a durable consequence of the ad cut, and that the organic channels (Branded Directory and Marketplace) are carrying the channel without the paid layer.

PProvider productivity

The productivity view (medical only) measures provider efficiency — visits per scheduled hour (VPH) — rather than demand. Where the volume stories above use visits per operating-day, this section uses VPH, which normalizes for staffed hours rather than calendar days. June extended the practice's strongest efficiency run, with the new provider now in her second month and ramping.

System VPH set another series high — now with hours back to normal

System VPH edged up to 5.71 in June — a new series high, up 0.7% from May and a striking 17.7% year-over-year. What makes June the more convincing record is that it came with scheduled hours back to a normal 1,974 (up 9.4% from May's Memorial-Day-shortened month). May's high was set on reduced hours; June sustained an even higher mark on a full staffed schedule — a cleaner signal of genuine efficiency. It also absorbed new provider Aileen Espinal, now in her second month and ramping (VPH 3.40, up from 3.08), which pulls the system average down; the established base is running higher still.

The 2025 cohort: Garcia broke through as Huang and Sit paused

The four-provider 2025 cohort showed a more mixed month. Ortiz reached a new high of 7.09, and — most notably — Garcia (GG) finally cleared the 3.5 Month-6 target, jumping to 3.65, her best month and the fourth consecutive month of gains after a long flat stretch. Against that, Huang eased from May's benchmark-crossing 5.12 back to 4.71, and Sit slipped from 4.80 to 4.65 — the first pullbacks for both after long climbs. Both remain on strong overall trajectories; a single down month is not a trend, but it is worth watching next cycle.

The veteran capacity gap remains the larger opportunity

Beneath the cohort story, the spread among established providers remains the bigger structural lever. On the trailing 3-month average (Apr–Jun), several high-hour veterans run below the system VPH, splitting into two patterns: a leaky-panel group (Shin, Kwak, Huang, Sit — high no-show rates of 25–27% despite solid throughput) and a tight-throughput group (Yu, Huh — healthy no-show but lower visit density). The two need different fixes — no-show recovery for the first, scheduling-template review for the second. Note that this same leaky-panel pattern ties directly to the Bronx Concourse no-show story above: high-NS providers and the high-NS site are the same underlying reliability problem viewed two ways. (Aileen Espinal, in her second month at 3.24 3-mo avg, sits low but is expected there as a ramping hire, not part of this established gap.)

Two problems, two remedies. Bundling all below-benchmark providers under "low productivity" obscures which lever to pull. The leaky-panel group's bookings are already made — the fix is getting patients to show, and it overlaps with the Bronx Concourse no-show intervention. The tight-throughput group shows up fine — the fix is visit density during booked time. The combined gap, if closed to system VPH, is worth several hundred visits per month at zero additional staffed hours.
