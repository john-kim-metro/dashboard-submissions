Office of the CIO/CSO — May 2026 Medical & Productivity Review

Author: John Kim · Reporting period: May 2026 · Prepared: June 8, 2026 · Status: Confidential
Companion analysis to the Medical Volume & No-Show and Medical Productivity dashboards

1Most of May's decline was a lost working day, not lost demand

Completed visits fell to 12,264, down 2.6% from April and 6.0% against May 2025. But May had fewer operating days. The practice is open full days Monday–Friday and half-days on Saturday, and it was closed for Memorial Day (Monday, May 25). Counting each weekday as 1.0 and each Saturday as 0.5, May 2026 had 22.5 operating-day-equivalents versus 24.0 in April and 23.5 in May 2025 (May 2025 had its own Memorial Day closure).

Normalizing to that basis tells the truer story: May ran 545 visits per operating-day (VPD), the metric now carried on the dashboard alongside raw volume — up 3.9% from April's 524 and only 1.8% below May 2025's 555. On a like-for-like capacity basis the practice was busier than April, and the year-over-year softness is modest. Restating May at April's 24.0 operating days implies roughly 13,080 visits — right in line with the recent run rate.

This operating-day lens matters because the raw monthly totals understate May across every location and category in this report — each carries the same reduced-capacity drag of roughly 6% versus April. Where a category or site is described below as down month-over-month, about 6 points of that is the calendar. The residual after that adjustment is the part worth acting on.

What this means: May was not a demand problem. Adjusted for operating days (half-day Saturdays included), per-day volume rose above April and nearly matched last May. June returns to a fuller operating-day count, so the raw monthly total should rebound mechanically — watch that it returns toward the ~13,000 the per-day rate implies, which would confirm the underlying trend is stable.

2The no-show rate hit a series high, led by Bronx Concourse

May's system no-show rate was 20.2% — up 1.6 points from April and the highest in the entire dataset back to January 2023. Unlike the volume figures, this is a ratio and is not distorted by the Memorial Day closure: fewer operating days removes both the numerator and denominator, so the 20.2% is a clean, real deterioration. This is the eighth consecutive month above 18%, and May pushed it to a new high.

Treating them as one "Bronx problem" will misdiagnose at least one of them.

3Procedures fell again — the decline is now eight months deep

Procedures dropped to 1,951 completed visits in May, down 23.3% year-over-year. This extends the structural decline documented since late 2025; it is now the steepest YoY drop in the run. The pattern across sub-types matches the prior diagnosis: payer-driven declines in the laser procedures, plus the continued SAAS erosion.

SAAS continued to weaken, now −27.5% YoY — a deeper drop than April's. Fewer operating days explain only ~6 points of this; a ~21% structural decline remains after the calendar adjustment, so this is a real trend, not an artifact. As established last month, SAAS is recorded under "Non-Provider, Treatment" so the ordering provider is not visible in the appointment data, and the decline concentrates at Bronx Fordham and Flushing Northern. The recommended EMA ordering-provider drill-down at those two sites remains the open action item from April; May's further decline raises its priority.

This is now a material revenue line. SAAS has fallen from roughly 1,400/month a year ago to barely 1,000 today. Combined with the payer-driven laser declines, the procedures category is running about 600 visits/month below its 2024 level. Unlike the laser declines, the SAAS portion is not explained by payer rules and remains the most actionable piece — if the ordering-pattern shift can be reversed, it is recoverable volume.

4Cutting ZocDoc Sponsored Ads coincided with a sharp drop in new patients

This is the month's most important signal on patient acquisition. ZocDoc Sponsored Ads were switched off in May after running ~80–90 bookings/month through April. In the same month, ZocDoc new-patient bookings fell 27% — from 696 in April to 505 in May, the steepest single-month drop of the year. The timing is hard to ignore: the one deliberate change to the channel coincided with its sharpest new-patient decline.

Notably, the drop (−191 new-patient bookings) is larger than the ~83 bookings the ads directly delivered in April. That suggests the ads may have been doing more than their face value — lifting overall discovery on the platform, not just the bookings attributed to them — though a single month cannot prove that. Other factors could be compounding. What is clear is the direction and the size: this is a material hit to the practice's largest new-patient channel, and it lines up precisely with the ad cut.

Monitor before concluding — but treat this as the leading hypothesis. One month is not proof that cutting Sponsored Ads caused the new-patient drop, but the timing, direction, and outsized magnitude all point that way. PProvider productivity

The productivity view (medical only) measures provider efficiency — visits per scheduled hour (VPH) — rather than demand. Where the volume stories above use visits per operating-day, this section uses VPH, which normalizes for staffed hours rather than calendar days. May was a strong month on this measure, and the practice added a new provider mid-stream.

A record system VPH — even while onboarding a new provider

System VPH reached 5.67 in May — the highest in the entire series, up 5.2% from April and 8.4% year-over-year. What makes the record notable is that it held up while the practice onboarded a new provider, Aileen Espinal, whose first-month VPH of 3.08 pulls the system average down. Absent her ramp, the established providers were running even higher. The Memorial Day closure also trimmed total scheduled hours (1,804, down 6.3% MoM), so the system delivered its strongest efficiency of the series on slightly reduced staffed time.

The veteran capacity gap remains the larger opportunity

Beneath the cohort story, the spread among established providers is the bigger structural lever. On the trailing 3-month average (Mar–May), several high-hour veterans run well below the system VPH, splitting into two distinct patterns: a leaky-panel group (Sit, Huang, Kwak — high no-show rates of 24–28%) and a tight-throughput group (Yu, Huh, Park Chohee — healthy no-show but lower visit density). The two need different fixes — no-show recovery for the first, scheduling-template review for the second. (Aileen Espinal, new in May at VPH 3.08, also sits low on the matrix, but as a first-month ramp she is expected there and is not part of this established-provider gap.)

Two problems, two remedies. Bundling all below-benchmark providers under "low productivity" obscures which lever to pull. The leaky-panel group's bookings are already made — the fix is getting patients to show. The tight-throughput group shows up fine — the fix is visit density during booked time. The combined gap, if closed to system VPH, is worth several hundred visits per month at zero additional staffed hours.
