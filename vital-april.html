Urgent-care needs book fast; elective and procedural ones linger.

Biologics patients are the fastest to act (median ~3.4 days) — these are active treatment patients who can't lapse. Accutane (~6.4d) and core Follow-Ups (~9d) follow. At the slow end, VBeam recalls (~21d), Walk-In conversions (~18d), and Zocdoc New (~15d) reflect more elective, lower-urgency decisions where patients deliberate before committing. The takeaway: responsiveness tracks clinical urgency, not the recall reason — useful for timing follow-up nudges per service line.

Does the VBeam campaign lead to VBeam appointments? No.

This is the clearest finding. Of the 5 VBeam recalls in the window, zero booked a VBeam or laser appointment — 4 booked a standard medical Follow-Up and 1 a Cosmetic Consult. The same pattern holds across every campaign: regardless of the recall reason (No-Show, 10-Day Follow-Up, Cancelled), roughly 60–80% of resulting bookings collapse into the generic Follow-Up type. Recalls are excellent at getting patients back through the door, but the recall reason does not predict the service booked. Treat campaigns as re-engagement drivers, not as demand signals for a specific service line.

Provider loyalty varies enormously — from 0% to 82% leakage.

At the clinic level, retention looks healthy (~95% rebook at the same office), but the provider view tells a different story. Several high-volume mid-levels lose most of their recalled patients to other clinicians: Mamanova (82%), Huang (73%), and Sit (68%) sit at the top. By contrast, Ortiz retains 77% of 66 recalls and Hu holds 100% of 11. Some leakage is structural — patients rebooking with a supervising physician, or PAs sharing a panel — but the spread is wide enough to be worth a workflow look. Note: provider-to-provider "leakage" within the same office isn't necessarily lost revenue; it matters most for continuity of care and panel attribution.

Method & assumptions
· Scope: Apr 1–20, 2026 recalls; all booked within 30 days of recall
· Success metric: appointment generated (future appt loaded)
· Medical revenue: flat $120/visit, reduced 30% for no-shows/cancels
· Cost: full $4,000/mo VI platform fee (no allocation)
· "Responsiveness" = appt booked (loaded) date − message sent date

Open items worth resolving next: (1) adding an EHR Patient ID to the export would let us join to billing/visit records and replace the 30% estimate with exact completed-visit ROI; (2) complete earlier-month data would give a fully-matured picture rather than a 20-day partial; (3) the $4,000 also powers confirmations, reminders, surveys, and cosmetic bulk-SMS — allocating only a share to recalls would raise the ROI further.
