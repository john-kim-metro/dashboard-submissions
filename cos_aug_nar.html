Metro Dermatology — August 2026 Cosmetic Review

Author: Darell Dado· Reporting period: August 2026· Prepared: September 11, 2026· Status: Confidential
Companion analysis to the Cosmetic Volume & No-Show dashboard
Cosmetic has a demand problem, not a conversion problem — and August makes that unusually clear. Completed visits fell to 862, −6.1% on July and −10.1% year-on-year: the lowest August in the three-year series. Yet the share of booked appointments that converted into a completed visit reached 74.3% — the highest figure in the entire series. Both failure rates improved. The book is simply smaller: total demand fell −10.1% on the month and −10.7% on the year. Nothing about how the practice handles a cosmetic booking is going wrong; fewer are being made.

1The smallest August in three years, on the best conversion in the series

August and July carried the same 23.5 operating-day-equivalents, so the raw and per-day moves are identical and there is no calendar effect to unwind. Visits per operating-day fell from 39.1 to 36.7.

What makes this month diagnostic is that every quality measure moved the right way at once. No-show rate improved from 6.5% to 5.7%. Cancellation rate improved from 23.8% to 21.2% — the lowest of 2026. Conversion hit 74.3%, above last August's 73.8% and the best month on record. The funnel is working better than it ever has; there is less going into it.

2The April event bought a spike, not a step change — now confirmed twice

April 2026 produced 1,227 completed cosmetic visits, the highest month in the series, on the back of the Jeuveau/Letybo event. The open question since has been whether that represented new recurring demand or a pulled-forward one-off. July was the first clean test and read as a one-off; August is the second and confirms it.

Volume has now fallen for two consecutive months to a level 30% below the April peak and below the equivalent month in both prior years. Year-to-date, 2026 is averaging 993 completed cosmetic visits a month against 1037 for the same period in 2025 — −4.3%. The event moved volume between months rather than raising the baseline.

Four of five locations are down year-on-year. Only Glen Oaks is ahead, at +27.2% YoY, and it is the smallest cosmetic site in the system. This is a system-wide softening, not one underperforming location dragging an otherwise healthy book.

3New patients are up; repeat visits are what fell

Read the category split as guidance, not an exact count. A booking carries only one appointment type, but a single visit can be more than one thing — a new patient can arrive as a walk-in and have a consultation, and only one of those gets recorded. The categories therefore borrow from one another. Use the figures below to see where movement is happening; they are not exact counts of each activity.

The category split contradicts the obvious reading. New Patient rose +23.0% on the month to 91 visits and is +18.2% year-on-year — the strongest category in the book and a full reversal of July's −32.1% collapse, which was flagged as an open question last cycle. Acquisition is not the problem.

Consult & Follow-up is. It fell −23.1% on the month to 100 visits and is −25.4% year-on-year — the largest decline of any category on both horizons. Consults and follow-ups are where a cosmetic patient is converted into a course of treatment. New patients arriving while consults fall is the signature of a funnel that is narrowing after the first appointment, not at the door.

Injectables held up (+2.5% MoM, −8.2% YoY) and Laser & Energy softened (−8.2% MoM). Walk-in, the largest single category, fell −11.2%.

4Bronx Concourse is the acute problem

Cosmetic at Bronx Concourse fell to 95 completed visits — −34.5% on the month and the same −34.5% year-on-year. It also carries the worst cosmetic no-show rate in the system at 20.8%, against a system average of 5.7%.

The same site is declining on the medical book at the same time. Medical at Bronx Concourse is −6.9% MoM and −10.5% YoY with a no-show rate of 27.5%. Two structurally independent books falling together at one location points to something site-specific — catchment, staffing, scheduling or premises — rather than anything about cosmetic demand. This pattern has now been observed for three consecutive cycles. It warrants a single coordinated site review covering both books, not two separate service-line investigations.

Glen Oaks is the one bright spot and is worth understanding. At +27.2% year-on-year it is the only cosmetic site growing, in a month when the system fell −10.1%. It opened during 2025, so part of this is a ramp effect — but it is the only place in the cosmetic book where something is working, and no operational driver was reported to explain it.

What to do with this

  1. Stop treating cosmetic as a conversion problem. No-show, cancellation and conversion are all at or near their best levels in the series. Effort spent on reminders and scheduling discipline will not recover this volume — the bookings are not being made in the first place.
  2. Investigate the consult-to-treatment step. New Patient is +18.2% YoY while Consult & Follow-up is −25.4%. Patients are arriving and not returning. That is the single most actionable finding in this report.
  3. Reset the post-event baseline. Two clean months now confirm the April Jeuveau/Letybo spike did not raise the underlying run-rate. Cosmetic planning should use roughly 993 visits a month, not the April figure.
  4. Open a coordinated Bronx Concourse site review. Cosmetic −34.5% YoY and medical −10.5% YoY at the same location, across three cycles.
  5. Ask what is working at Glen Oaks. The only growing cosmetic site in the system, with no reported operational driver.
